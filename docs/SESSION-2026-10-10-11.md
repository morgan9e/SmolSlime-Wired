## Outcome in one paragraph

**The original problem was not fixed.** The problem is that one tracker at a time
stops updating for 400 ms to several seconds while the others keep working, which
freezes that body part in full-body tracking. The session established that
these freezes are real radio delivery failures, not idle suppression. They move
between trackers over time and depend strongly on pose and placement. It also
built an experimental TDMA scheduler, wireless TDMA/legacy switching and a
radio-only synthetic test firmware, all OTA-capable. A hardware-timer scheduler
removed TDMA's own missed-slot bug. In the last
controlled measurement, legacy ESB had **no gap ≥400 ms on any ProMicro**.
TDMA had **10 such intervals on 91 in the first stage (worst 2.39 s) and 5 in the
repeat (worst 11.67 s)**; the other six stayed ≤60 ms in both modes. The 0.0.30
fix for that was only partly installed when the receiver disconnected, and it
was never measured. The user then asked for a rollback to stock firmware plus
the custom OTA. **That rollback has not been done.**

> **Last known devices are running radio-only test firmware that sends no body
> poses.** SlimeVR tracking will not work until normal firmware is restored over
> OTA. See [last known device state](#last-known-device-state).

The firmware was also checked while analyzing these runs:

- **Idle logic:** a tracker sends orientation only if the quaternion moves more
  than `0.001` (component comparison) or linear acceleration changes more than `0.05`.
  This is a software decision after the IMU reading. After **500 ms** of detected
  inactivity it drops to a slower sensor loop. Wake-on-motion sleep can follow
  after **5–15 s** by default. Packet generation and rest detection share one
  reference orientation, so slow motion can look "resting"
  ([sensor.c rest check](../Tracker/src/sensor/sensor.c:594), [thresholds](../Tracker/src/sensor/sensor.c:1097)).
  This was never shown to cause a recorded silence.
- **Sleep report decoding:** packets 6 and 7 carry a sleep countdown. The field is
  `uint16`, but the firmware assigns an int64 timeout without saturation, so long
  values wrap modulo 65536 (observed values ~38,400–48,024; 65535 means no
  deadline). No left-ankle report ever approached zero.
- **Update rate:** the "100 Hz" setting is a 10 ms sensor-loop target. FIFO rounding
  gives roughly **120 updates/s** on the ProMicro IMU. Before the patch the
  trackers sent about 220–240/s. **Legacy mode has no hard 100 Hz transmit
  cap.** Only TDMA enforces one, at one slot per 10 ms frame.
- **Retry delays:** the per-device retry delay patch derives a delay from the
  address. **IDs 1, 3 and 7 all compute to 600 µs**, so it does not guarantee
  de-synchronized retries.
- Nordic quotes about **−89 dBm** sensitivity at 2 Mbps. The −70 dBm links
  were not too weak in principle; collisions or interference can still corrupt them.

**Conclusions at this stage:** loss happens while sequence numbers keep
advancing, so it is not idle suppression. Receiver CRC failures ranged
**18–70%**. The bad ankle **did not follow the side of the body**: crossing the
legs brought both ankles to ~11%. The A/B/A orientation test **did not
reproduce**: flat → upright → flat went 18.2% → 44.8% → 69.6% CRC failures, so
orientation was not shown to be the cause. Receiver-only data cannot separate
tracker-to-tracker collisions, outside interference and tracker-side behaviour.

### 4. TDMA prototype (10-11, 05:49, `ede7e4e`)

Requested in a side chat. The receiver beacons a **10 ms frame with eight 1 ms
slots**: first slot at 1 ms, 200 µs guard, transmit start allowed only within a
**150 µs window** and prepared 700 µs ahead. Each tracker sends its newest pending
frame in its slot, so at most 100 per second. A missing beacon or late wakeup
skips the slot. There are no retransmissions, the radio listens more, and the
**firmware OTA path was disabled in TDMA builds** (see §8). It was opt-in at build time
(`make tdma`, outputs in `dist/tdma/`) and build-tested only at that point.

### 8. OTA had been disabled in TDMA; restored (10-11, ~16:00–16:35)

The user asked for a stationary **bench mode**: `bench on/off/status`, volatile and
off after reboot, keeping the sensor at active rate and sending every cycle.
They also asked for it to be installed over OTA. It then emerged that **the
0.0.19 TDMA tracker firmware ignores OTA offers, even in legacy mode**. The TDMA
prototype had disabled OTA to avoid conflicts with the scheduler, and this was
not raised before flashing. The user was rightly angry; wireless updates had
been a hard requirement.

Restoration: OTA takes exclusive radio ownership, the receiver pauses beacons
during the transfer, and board checks, CRC validation and MCUboot rollback are
unchanged.

### 10. Radio-only synthetic firmware (10-11, 17:18–17:40)

This was the user's idea: an OTA-installed firmware that sends test packets
independent of the IMU. It stops IMU sampling and fusion, generates one
**21-byte packet every 10 ms** with pseudorandom payload and sequence numbers, and
keeps pairing, MCUboot and OTA. Counters report generated, generator-skipped,
TDMA late, submitted, replaced, ACKed, failed and API errors. The receiver
counts these packets but never forwards them to SlimeVR.

- **0.0.26** (`f5d276a`/`8f585b5`): 8A received it over OTA, then went silent
  and came back still reporting confirmed 0.0.25, so MCUboot had rolled it
  back. The cause was the new sensor-shutdown call running on a **256-byte
  startup stack**. **0.0.27** (`86c04ac`) moved it to the 1 KB sensor worker.
  8A booted it, confirmed, then made an OTA round trip to 0.0.25 and back. Per
  the user's instruction, **USB was used only to read diagnostics**.
- 0.0.27 went to 7A, 59, C8, 20, 8A and 78 entirely by OTA. 91 was "not recently
  heard" and was skipped (it stayed on normal 0.0.25). The user chose to **leave
  radio-only firmware installed**.
- **17:36 comparison** (appendix I).
  Generator skips and API errors were 0. In TDMA, trackers **missed ~18–51 send
  windows per second** before ever submitting a packet. For 78, delivery per
  diagnostic second went **43.6–50.6 → 89.6–98.0 (legacy) → 45.2–49.6** with
  ~44–51 late slots/s, a **TDMA scheduler limitation that does not depend on the IMU**.
  Separately, **59 had 882 / 507 / 332 ms gaps** in TDMA1, versus a 91 ms max in legacy.
  91 appeared from ~6.7 to 11.7 s, and E4 from 24.9 s onward.

Why the slots were missed: a beacon timestamp came from a software callback,
then a worker polled the clock with `k_usleep(100)` and reconfigured the radio
near the slot. Zephyr does not guarantee that wake-up time. The standard fix is
to capture the beacon in hardware and start TX from a hardware timer through
PPI.

**0.0.30** (`0791163`, tag `0.0.30`):

- **Beacon holdover:** a tracker keeps sending in its slot through missed beacons
  for up to **500 ms** (`TDMA_HOLDOVER_US`), with a tested drift margin, then stops
  if sync does not return. The receiver keeps a fixed beacon grid when it skips a
  preparation.
- **Retained latest update:** the newest frame stays pending until ACKed, so a
  failed final movement update is not dropped when the tracker goes still.
- New counters: beacons, holdover TX, empty slots, missed beacons, unsynced
  pending and retained failures. There is a gap reporter (`tools/radio_gap_report.py`)
  and tests (`tests/test_radio_recovery.c`).

## What was established

1. Freezes are **real delivery failures** with sequence numbers still advancing.
   They are not explained by idle suppression or sleep.
2. Failures **move between trackers and over time** and depend strongly on pose,
   obstruction and placement. The "bad left ankle" did not follow the side of the
   body, and 7A swung between the worst and a fine tracker across runs.
3. Receiver-wide **CRC failure was 18–70%** while worn. A background scan found
   intermittent bursts up to −33 dBm on 2402 MHz with our transmitters muted.
4. Worker-thread TDMA **missed ~18–51 slots/s**. TIMER1/PPI brought that to **0**.
5. With accurate slots, TDMA's single attempt and lack of beacon holdover made
   it **worse than legacy for freezes on the weakest link (91)**. Legacy had no
   ≥400 ms gap in the 0.0.29 baseline.
6. OTA works robustly: 22–29 s per ~221 KB image, with wrong-board refusal,
   interrupted-transfer recovery and MCUboot rollback all exercised on hardware.

## What was not established

- The cause of the worn-session CRC failures: tracker collisions, outside
  interference or weak links in any given proportion.
- Whether 0.0.30 reduces freezes. It was never measured.
- Any result at the target **5–7 m**, or with all trackers worn on battery since
  the 10-10 captures.
- Antenna hardware faults. None were shown, and none were ruled out for specific units.
- Why 78 disappeared for 118.75 s and 18–31 s on 10-10, and why 8A went silent
  after two OTA boots.

## Mistakes and lessons recorded

- **Firmware OTA was disabled in the TDMA prototype** without raising it, which forced
  USB repairs. Requirements such as OTA must never be removed silently.
- The first mode switch **required every paired tracker**, so an absent XIAO blocked
  it. Fixed in 0.0.20.
- The 0.0.26 shutdown call **overflowed a 256-byte stack**, which MCUboot rolled back.
- The 10 ms sensor patch was described as a "100 Hz airtime cap". **Legacy has no
  transmit cap.**
- Modes were compared by **average delivered packets**, and legacy was recommended
  on that basis. **Judge by per-tracker gap distribution (≥50/100/200/400/1000 ms).**
- Intermediate results (e.g., hardware timer) were presented as progress toward the
  user's actual goal before that was shown.
- Short, unpaired captures with pose, placement and orientation all changing produced
  non-reproducible conclusions (A/B/A). Hold everything fixed but one variable.
