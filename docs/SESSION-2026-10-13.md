### 5. IMU probe firmware and the 6D brick (14:58–15:19)

The user asked for "a very very simple custom firmware" that checks for an IMU on
SPI. The assistant wrote `ImuProbe/`, a standalone Zephyr app for
`promicro_uf2/nrf52840` with a `make imu-probe` target. It:
- power-cycles the IMU through P0.17/P0.20;
- tests MISO with pull-up/pull-down, with CS high and low;
- bit-bangs SPI to read the ID registers in modes 3 and 0 (0x0F for LSM6, 0x75/0x72
  for ICM, 0x00 for BMI270).

It built to 40 KB, and the UF2 reported `start address: 0x1000`.

- **15:00, first copy onto 6DF7 (no confirmation asked).** UF2 range
  0x1000–0xAE00 was checked against the settings area. The board never came back on
  USB. Cause found: the build used the Zephyr default
  `CONFIG_CLOCK_CONTROL_NRF_K32SRC_XTAL=y` with `SYSTEM_CLOCK_WAIT_FOR_STABILITY`.
  The tracker uses `CONFIG_CLOCK_CONTROL_NRF_K32SRC_RC=y` (`Tracker/prj.conf:72`)
  because these boards have no 32 kHz crystal.
- **15:02.** The assistant rebuilt with RC LFCLK and started a background watcher
  (`bsh279xar`, 15-min wait) to auto-copy the probe when a bootloader drive appeared.
  It asked the user to double-tap reset. From 15:04 to 15:12 the user sent "." four
  times and no drive appeared.
- **15:12.** The user asked: *"how did you even managed to completely brick it"*. The
  assistant took responsibility: untested firmware was flashed without asking. It
  explained that the bootloader (0xF4000) and settings (0xEE000) were untouched and
  gave RST-to-GND double-tap instructions.
- **15:16, second copy.** 6DF7 appeared as `E:` (bootloader 0.11.0). Both the
  assistant's foreground script and the watcher copied the RC-clock probe:
  `probe did not enumerate`. **readelf** then showed the real fault: the ELF LOAD
  segment was at **0x00000000**. The NCS partition manager had placed `app` at 0x0
  (size 0x100000), even though `CONFIG_FLASH_LOAD_OFFSET=0x1000`. The 0x1000 start in
  the UF2 had hidden this.
- **15:18, fix and third copy.** Copying `Tracker/pm_static/nrf52840_uf2.yml` to
  `ImuProbe/pm_static.yml` moved the app to 0x1000–0xF4000 (LOAD 0x00001000, reset

  user then rejected the assistant's memory-index edit and interrupted ("STOP ...

- **15:19.** The watcher ended with exit 1. Its output (read at 21:02) was

## What was established

1. `IMU: None` on 6DF7, 7A93 and 74DA was present **before** this session's reflash.
   The same stock SPI image detects the IMU on DE/D2/04/84/2D, so the stock SPI
   firmware and pin map work on this board type.
2. **7A93 and 74DA detect their LSM6DSV on the stock I2C build.** 74DA later also
   works on SPI. 7A93 still does not.
3. **For the LSM6DSV, CS selects the interface:** high is I2C, low is SPI. The user's
   I2C success with CS held at 3.3 V (22:02) and the I2C build's success on 7A93/74DA
   are consistent with this. The I2C build's board file has no CS pin.
4. **6DF7's IMU never answered.** It failed on stock SPI, on stock I2C with CS
   externally at 3.3 V, and from the RP2040, even though its module pull-ups were
   present and SCK/MOSI pulses reached its pads.
5. **The RP2040 method works on a known-good board:** D235 answered 0x6B / `0x70` over
   I2C and was found in both SPI modes by the probe.
6. **A standalone ProMicro app needs two things:** the 0x1000 partition map
   (`pm_static.yml`) and RC LFCLK. The UF2's reported start address does not prove the
   link address; readelf does.
7. **The stock `cbfff16` source does not compile for `stackedi2c`** without the
   `253c08c` fix. The CI I2C image the user had was built from `aa93e5c`.
8. **Device addresses are chip-bound** (FICR). Tracker IDs are pairing slots.

## Mistakes and lessons recorded

- **Untested firmware was flashed without asking.** The probe left 6D with no USB, and
  the user had to recover it. Two separate build faults caused it: the XTAL LFCLK
  default, and the partition manager linking at 0x0. A background watcher also
  auto-copied a still-unverified image. **Ask before flashing anything new, verify the
  link address with readelf, test on one board first, and don't leave auto-flashing
  watchers running.**
- **A swallowed `FileNotFoundError`** (`except OSError: pass` around the UF2 copy)
  left two trackers in the bootloader with a misleading "did not come back". Copy
  errors other than the expected drive-eject must surface.
- **Tests were run without knowing which board was wired.** The RP2040 was on board
  "98" while 74DA was being probed. The assistant drew conclusions from that (74DA's
  "dead supply") and had to retract them. **Confirm the wired board, by address,
  before each test.**
- **Conflicting drivers on CS:** the user's 3.3 V on CS was in place while stock SPI
  firmware (7A93) or the probe's SPI pass pulled CS low. Those SPI results were
  invalid. Use the I2C build or the hold probe whenever CS is tied externally, ideally
  through ~1 kΩ.
- **Back-powering:** an unpowered tracker gave a half-alive I2C ACK (`0x6B`, ID
  `0x00`) through the RP2040's pull-ups and the 3.3 V on CS. **Apply external signals
  only to a powered tracker.** The assistant said it should have warned sooner.
- **Over-confident diagnoses:** "74DA's chip is dead", "I2C-style modules" and "6DF7's
  CS is shorted" were each stated firmly and later revised. The CS-and-I2C explanation
  confused the user.
- **The USB plug watcher was stopped by mistake** mid-test and restarted.
- **The memory-index edit was rejected**, and the user interrupted to stop the work at
  15:18. The assistant then stopped and waited.
- **`git push` was blocked** by the auto-mode classifier. It went through after the
  user explicitly approved.
